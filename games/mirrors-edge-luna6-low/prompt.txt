# Build a Mirror's Edge–Inspired Browser Parkour Game

## Project Overview

Create a fully playable **3D first-person parkour game** inspired by the movement mechanics, level design, and visual aesthetic of Mirror's Edge.

The game must run directly in a modern desktop browser using **Three.js and WebGL**.

The experience should feel fast, fluid, responsive, and satisfying. Players will sprint, jump, slide, vault, and wall-run across a minimalist futuristic city made of white rooftops, red architectural elements, and glass skyscrapers.

**Important:** This should be an actual playable game with a complete level, objectives, checkpoints, and a finish line — not just a 3D environment or movement demonstration.

Use an original environment and original level design inspired by the genre, rather than recreating copyrighted game assets or levels.

---

## 1. Technology Stack

- Three.js for 3D rendering
- TypeScript for game logic
- Vite for development and bundling
- WebGL renderer
- Pointer Lock API for FPS camera controls
- Custom lightweight player physics and collision detection
- CSS/HTML for menus, HUD, and screen overlays

Avoid unnecessary frameworks, complicated dependencies, and external game engines.

Prioritize smooth performance, clear code architecture, and immediate playability.

## 2. Visual Direction

The visual aesthetic is extremely important.

Create a bright, minimalist futuristic city inspired by Mirror's Edge.

### Art Style
- Predominantly white architecture
- Bright blue sky
- Clean geometric buildings
- Occasional vibrant red objects to guide navigation
- Subtle gray surfaces and concrete textures
- Reflective blue-tinted glass skyscrapers
- Strong natural sunlight
- Soft ambient shadows
- Slight atmospheric haze for distant buildings
- High contrast between white rooftops and red parkour elements
- Minimal visual clutter

### Environment
The game takes place on rooftops high above a large modern city.

Include:
- Rooftops at different heights
- Narrow bridges between buildings
- Large gaps that require running jumps
- Red ventilation ducts
- Air conditioning units and small obstacles
- Climbable ledges
- Wall-running surfaces
- Rooftop railings
- Glass-covered office buildings
- Distant skyscrapers forming a city skyline
- Occasional red doors, pipes, panels, and platforms

Use simple geometric meshes and procedural materials where possible. Avoid relying on external models or textures.

### Lighting and Rendering
- Directional sunlight with realistic shadows
- Ambient or hemisphere lighting
- Soft shadows and ambient depth
- Atmospheric distance fog
- Tone mapping and correct color management
- Optional subtle bloom, only if performance allows
- Clean anti-aliased rendering

The scene should look intentionally designed and visually appealing, not like a collection of random cubes.

## 3. First-Person Player Controller

The player movement system is the most important part of the entire game.

Movement must feel responsive, smooth, fast, and momentum-based.

### Basic Controls

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| WASD | Move |
| Mouse | Look around |
| Shift | Sprint |
| Space | Jump |
| Ctrl / C | Slide |
| E | Vault / interact |
| R | Restart from checkpoint |
| Esc | Pause / unlock mouse |

### Movement Physics

Implement:
- Smooth acceleration and deceleration
- Momentum conservation
- Sprinting with increased maximum speed
- Air movement and limited air control
- Gravity and falling
- Collision detection against world geometry
- Proper ground detection
- Variable jump height based on jump button duration
- Coyote time for forgiving jumps
- Jump buffering
- Controlled friction when landing

Movement should feel fast and athletic instead of floaty.

The player should be able to chain parkour moves together while maintaining momentum.

## 4. Parkour Mechanics

Implement the following mechanics, prioritizing reliability and good movement feel.

### A. Running and Sprinting
- Responsive WASD movement
- Sprinting increases movement speed
- Smooth acceleration to top speed
- Preserve speed while navigating obstacles
- Slightly increase camera field of view at high speeds

### B. Jumping
- Jump while standing or sprinting
- Sprinting allows longer jumps because horizontal momentum is preserved
- Allow minor air steering
- Smooth landing behavior
- Camera reacts subtly to takeoff and landing

### C. Sliding
- Press Ctrl while running to slide
- Camera lowers toward the ground
- Player collision height becomes smaller
- Sliding preserves initial momentum
- Speed gradually decreases due to friction
- Player can slide underneath low obstacles
- Allow jumping out of a slide
- Return smoothly to standing height after sliding when there is enough headroom

### D. Wall Running
- Detect runnable vertical surfaces near the player
- Allow wall running when airborne and moving fast enough
- Support left-side and right-side wall runs
- Reduce the effect of gravity during the wall run
- Move the player along the wall
- Tilt the camera slightly toward the wall
- Allow jumping away from the wall
- Limit wall-running duration
- Prevent infinite wall-running exploits

Wall running should be useful for crossing specific rooftop gaps.

### E. Vaulting
- Detect small obstacles directly in front of the player
- Press E to vault them
- Smoothly move the player over the obstacle
- Preserve forward momentum
- Prevent vaulting through large walls
- Use brief procedural camera movement to communicate the action

### F. Ledge Recovery
- Detect reachable ledges when the player is just below the rooftop edge
- Allow a short, smooth mantle onto the platform
- Prevent clipping through obstacles

Prioritize consistent movement behavior over physically perfect simulation.

## 5. First-Person Camera Effects

The camera should communicate speed and physical movement without making the player uncomfortable.

Implement:
- Smooth mouse look
- Adjustable mouse sensitivity
- Subtle walking and sprinting head bob
- Speed-dependent field of view
- Slight camera roll while strafing
- Camera tilt during wall-running
- Small landing impact animation
- Smooth camera height transition during sliding
- Subtle camera animation during vaulting

Avoid excessive shaking or aggressive head bobbing.

Include an option to reduce camera motion.

## 6. Player Body Visualization

Even though the game is first-person, include subtle visual feedback from the player's body.

Examples:
- Simple stylized hands visible during certain moves
- Arms briefly appearing while vaulting
- Legs or feet visible when looking down, if feasible
- Procedural arm movement when sprinting

Use simple low-poly geometry.

Do not allow body meshes to obstruct the player's view.

These effects are lower priority than gameplay and should only be added after the core movement system works correctly.

## 7. Level Design

Create one complete, intentionally designed rooftop parkour level.

Target completion time: **60–120 seconds for a successful run**.

The level must have a clear start and finish.

### Level Structure

**Section 1 — Movement Tutorial**

A large starting rooftop where players learn:
- Running
- Sprinting
- Jumping
- Sliding

Include a small obstacle and a short gap.

**Section 2 — Rooftop Jumps**

Several rooftops separated by gaps.

Players must sprint and jump between buildings.

Place red visual indicators near important jump locations.

**Section 3 — Sliding Challenge**

A rooftop filled with low ventilation structures.

Players must slide underneath obstacles and maintain momentum.

**Section 4 — Wall Run**

Create a gap that cannot be crossed with a normal jump.

Place a long wall beside the gap.

The player must use wall running to reach the next platform.

**Section 5 — Parkour Combination**

Combine several actions:
- Sprint
- Jump
- Vault
- Wall run
- Slide

Make this section challenging but fair.

**Section 6 — Final Sprint**

A fast final rooftop sequence with several jumps and an obvious red finish marker.

Crossing the finish line completes the level.

### Level Design Rules
- The intended route must always be readable
- Highlight important interactive surfaces in red
- Make jumps physically possible using the implemented movement values
- Add enough landing space after difficult jumps
- Avoid invisible walls
- Avoid unfair obstacles
- Reward players for maintaining momentum
- Add checkpoints after major sections
- Falling off the rooftops causes respawning at the latest checkpoint

Place background buildings outside the playable level to give the illusion of a much larger city.

## 8. Runner Vision

Implement a simple navigation system inspired by Mirror's Edge's Runner Vision.

Important traversal objects should feature red accents.

Examples:
- Red pipes
- Red platforms
- Red wall-run surfaces
- Red vault obstacles
- Red rooftop doors
- Red finish markers

Do not paint the entire route red. Use red intentionally to guide the player's attention.

The route should also remain understandable through architecture and object placement.

### Main Menu

- Start Game button

- Mouse sensitivity setting
- Camera motion setting

### In-Game HUD
- Small minimal crosshair
- Current run timer
- Checkpoint notification
- Brief contextual control hints
- Pause menu

### Finish Screen
When the player crosses the finish line:
- Display "LEVEL COMPLETE"
- Show completion time
- Show best time saved locally
- Display a Restart button

Keep the HUD minimal, modern, and visually consistent with the game's aesthetic.

Use clean typography with white, black, gray, and red accents.

## 10. Sound Design

If feasible, generate lightweight sound effects with the Web Audio API.

Include:
- Footsteps
- Jump sound
- Landing sound
- Sliding sound
- Wind while sprinting
- Checkpoint activation
- Level completion sound

Do not require downloading large audio assets.

Sound should enhance movement feedback without becoming distracting.

## 11. Physics and Collision Reliability

Collision and physics correctness are essential.

Use a consistent fixed-timestep movement update independent of rendering frame rate.

Implement reliable collision handling for:
- Rooftop floors
- Walls
- Platforms
- Obstacles
- Low ceilings
- Wall-running geometry
- Vault targets
- Ledges

Make sure:
- The player cannot fall through floors
- The player cannot walk through solid walls
- High-speed movement does not easily tunnel through obstacles
- Respawning resets velocity and movement state
- Sliding does not cause the player to get stuck
- Jumping and landing behave consistently at different frame rates

Keep the collision system simple but robust.

Use readable TypeScript, descriptive names, and clear comments for complex movement logic.

## 13. Performance

The game should run smoothly in desktop Chrome, Firefox, and Edge.

Target 60 FPS on an average modern laptop.

Optimize with:
- Shared geometries and materials
- Instanced meshes for repeated background objects
- Sensible shadow map resolution
- Limited draw calls
- Reasonable pixel ratio
- Simple shaders and materials
- Efficient collision checks
- Proper disposal of resources

Do not introduce expensive visual effects unless they make a meaningful difference.

**Phase 2 — Parkour**

**Phase 3 — Complete Level**

## 15. Important Requirements

- The game must be playable immediately after running the development server.
- Implement actual mechanics, not placeholder methods.
- Do not substitute a static scene or video for gameplay.
- Do not rely on paid assets or external APIs.
- Do not require a backend.
- Do not add multiplayer, enemies, combat, or unnecessary gameplay systems.
- Prioritize movement feel and visual coherence.
- Use procedural geometry and materials whenever possible.
- Keep the overall scope manageable.
- Ensure the level is completable from start to finish.

## Final Quality Target

The result should feel like a small, polished indie parkour game.

The most important qualities, in order, are:

1. **Excellent movement feel** — fast, responsive, and momentum-based.
2. **Reliable parkour mechanics** — jumping, sliding, wall running, and vaulting.
3. **Strong visual identity** — bright white architecture, blue sky, striking red details.
4. **Thoughtful level design** — a continuous route across rooftops.
5. **Satisfying feedback** — subtle first-person camera effects and movement transitions.
6. **Solid performance** — smooth and lightweight browser execution.

Focus on making a simple experience feel genuinely good rather than creating a large but unfinished game.
